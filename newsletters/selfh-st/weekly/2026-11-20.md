## Weekly Highlights

**AI is ruining the internet**. I haven't been shy in the past about my disdain for how AI has re-shaped online interactions, whether it's the influx of bot accounts on social media or the dozens of emails I receive each week that are all some form of the following:

More specifically this week, though, I'm perturbed by how AI is closing off the open web after Reddit officially discontinued RSS feeds (they've been [talking about it for months](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/30/reddit-is-killing-rss-feeds-ending-public-api-access-because-of-ai-bots/?ref=selfh.st), but feeds finally stopped working this past Monday) and Last.fm announced [API access will require a paid account](https://support.last.fm/t/api-keys-will-require-pro-from-1st-december/121030?ref=selfh.st) starting in December.

Both of these announcements hit close to 127.0.0.1 given the number of platforms that [rely on Reddit's feeds](https://github.com/johnwarne/upvote-rss?ref=selfh.st) (mostly because of its dogsh*t interface) or Last.fm for the ability to [scrobble](https://selfh.st/apps/?tag=Scrobble) streams from self-hosted music sources.

But as frustrated as I am with the changes, I can't say I don't understand them. Services are realizing that if they don't charge for their content, AI companies will happily profit off it for free.

If our goal was ever to create an open internet, we're officially moving backwards.

In other news and activity:

- **Codeberg** [experienced downtime this week](https://social.anoxinon.de/@codebergstatus?ref=selfh.st) as a result of domain troubles that are still being [sorted out with their registrar](https://social.anoxinon.de/@codebergstatus/117637187711177698?ref=selfh.st)
- Two popular self-hosted projects were renamed – **Hister** (search) is now [Nogon](https://github.com/asciimoo/nogon?ref=selfh.st), and **Edit Mind** (video knowledge base) is now [Orcah Studio](https://github.com/IliasHad/orcah-studio?ref=selfh.st)
- **OpenCloud** announced they're [restricting production releases to enterprise customers](https://opencloud.eu/en/code-is-quick-to-generate-but-proper-maintenance-takes-time?ref=selfh.st) and limiting community users to rolling releases (similar to what [Proxmox](https://pve.proxmox.com/wiki/Package_Repositories?ref=selfh.st#sysadmin_enterprise_repo) and others do)
- **Infisical** *(disclosure: this week's sponsor)* took some heat for [paywalling custom roles](https://github.com/Infisical/infisical/commit/e4880e71edd3ecace288523a24da7e61d09b3ace?ref=selfh.st) in a recent patch release. Their founder [responded to the backlash](https://www.reddit.com/r/selfhosted/s/SHw3DxSs8C?ref=selfh.st) and acknowledged the lack of communication, but the paywall seems to be staying put.
- **BookOrbit **is [scaring off third-party developers](https://github.com/deranjer/bookorbit-android?ref=selfh.st#no-longer-maintained) with restricted API routes, which the [maintainer claims](https://github.com/bookorbit/bookorbit/issues/2143?ref=selfh.st#issuecomment-6752312659) is to thwart cracked versions of the official app ([please stop using this platform](https://selfh.st/weekly/2026-09-18/#weekly-highlights))
- **NordPass** released their [annual report of the most common passwords](https://nordpass.com/most-common-passwords-list/?ref=selfh.st), and (unsurprisingly) **123456** leads the pack (and apparently has for seven of the last eight years)
- FoxxMD, the maintainer of **Multi-Scrobbler**, is already working to help users migrate from Last.fm and [needs some testers](https://github.com/FoxxMD/multi-scrobbler/issues/791?ref=selfh.st)
- **Redict**, a popular fork of Redis [created over licensing concerns](https://redis.io/blog/redis-adopts-dual-source-available-licensing/?ref=selfh.st), [is being deprecated](https://redict.io/posts/2026-11-17-redict-deprecated/?ref=selfh.st) (and its maintainers are encouraging users to switch to [Valkey](https://valkey.io/?ref=selfh.st) instead)
- **Wiki.js** v3.0, which had [been in development for years](https://js.wiki/blog/2022-wiki-js-3-developer-preview/?ref=selfh.st) until its [recent release](https://js.wiki/blog/2026-wiki-js-3-release/?ref=selfh.st), is somehow already on [v3.5.0](https://github.com/requarks/wiki/releases/tag/3.5.0?ref=selfh.st)
- **Manjaro Linux**, a [semi-popular](https://selfh.st/survey/2026-results/#linux-distributions) Linux distribution, [officially registered as a recognized non-profit](https://forum.manjaro.org/t/the-manjaro-project-e-v-is-now-official/191197?ref=selfh.st) in Germany
- Novel project ideas I stumbled upon: Turn DNS queries from ad-blocking platforms into a [Galaga-style browser game](https://github.com/m00grin/ph-intercept?ref=selfh.st); convert a tablet into a [wall-mounted grandfather clock](https://hjertis.github.io/modern-clock/?ref=selfh.st) with notifications

## Content Spotlight

Meet **Tunarr**, a self-hosted platform for emulating custom TV channels. With Tunarr, users can recreate the broadcast TV experience with custom-scheduled channels of their own media that can be browsed via a traditional guide-like interface. Features include support for various media sources (local files, Jellyfin, Plex, Emby), advanced search and filters, filler content (commercials, music videos, etc.), and per-channel subtitles.

**Tunarr** can be [easily deployed via Docker](https://github.com/chrisbenincasa/tunarr?ref=selfh.st#quick-start) and doesn't require additional containers.

## Command Line Corner

Preface commands with **nohup** (no hang up) to continue running them after exiting a terminal session. Upon completion, **nohup** will create a file titled *nohup.out* with the output of the command for completion verification.

```bash
$ nohup apt update
  nohup: ignoring input and appending output to 'nohup.out'

$ cat nohup.out
  ...
  Reading package lists...
  Building dependency tree...
  Reading state information...
  6 packages can be upgraded.
```
